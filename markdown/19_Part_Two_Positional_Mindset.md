# Part Two: Positional Mindset

Part One built a vocabulary. Head and hip height, grips, posture and base, quadrants, the Konami Code, centerline, inside position, the strike zone, two-on-one, the process of submission, fulcrums and levers, spinal alignment, yin and yang, dilemmas, and action and reaction were intentionally taught as separate concepts independent of positions, though positions were often used as examples.

The chapters ahead move through positions, from open guard to the standing and scrambling exchanges, but they are not a catalog of techniques. Each position is a place to watch the same concepts show up again under new and sometimes more specific conditions. The question changes from "what is this idea" to "which of these ideas is deciding the exchange in front of me, and what does it tell me to do next." Keep the concept chapters within reach as you read. When a pass, a sweep, or an escape finally makes sense, it is almost always because one of the battles was won or lost before the movement began.

The chapters ahead share a structure, so you can find your way around any of them. Each one starts with the jobs: what the top player and the bottom player are each trying to do, or in some chapters the attacker and the defender, or the guard player and the passer. Most then run the position through the four battles from Part One, head height, hip height, grips, and posture and base, under a single heading, so you can compare how the same battle looks from closed guard, from mount, or from the back. Between those sections are the ideas that belong to that position alone, such as the height dilemma in mount or the safety concerns in leg entanglements. Most chapters close with Common Misreads, the mistakes students make most often in the position, and every chapter ends with a Training Observation, one thing to watch for in your next round.
