# Acknowledgments

Every belt and every stripe I have earned came from Ron Keslar. That lineage matters to me. Ron brought me into jiu jitsu, trained me privately before I moved fully into AKA, kept me connected to the academy even after my family moved, and ultimately tied the black belt around my waist. This book comes out of that history. My jiu jitsu game is a reflection of his, and I am proud beyond measure of my lineage.

American Kickboxing Academy and its team shaped me. The room and my teammates taught me humility, pressure, toughness, and honesty. I learned there as a student, taught there as an instructor, and absorbed lessons from more rounds, corrections, failures, and conversations than I could ever list cleanly.

Rudy Sanchez, owner of Smash Gyms, was instrumental to my growth and progression toward black belt. The two-on-one material in this book comes from him, but his influence is not limited to that one system. Multiple systems and ways of seeing positions trace back to Rudy's teaching and the time I spent learning from him.

Andrew Kerfoot, owner of Nexus Jiu Jitsu, left an indelible mark on how I think and teach. His conceptual approach aligned with the way my brain operated, and he opened new ways for me to organize positions, problems, and solutions, and new ways to communicate them.

I am grateful to my training partners, students, and coaches at AKA, Nexus Jiu Jitsu, and Refuge Jiu Jitsu. A person does not get better in isolation. Training partners expose what works, what fails, what needs to be rebuilt, and what needs to be understood more deeply.

I'd like to give special thanks to Josh Harsono, who spent countless hours reviewing every word, sentence, paragraph, and chapter in this book. Further, as a brown belt, he is a fantastic training partner, and everyone that has an opportunity to train with him gets a glimpse of his creativity, dedication to details, and relentlessly offensive style. Every move this man makes puts danger on his opponents. Thank you, Josh.

Finally, I am grateful to my family. Jiu jitsu asks for time, energy, attention, and sacrifice. None of this work happens without people at home supporting the person who keeps walking back onto the mats. My hope is that what I've learned and how I'm sharing it with others will give back to my family that so generously lends me the time and space to explore this deep passion of mine.
